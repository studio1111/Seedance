# Environment Design Reference

## Purpose

This document is the canonical environment specification for the complete 300-second Seedance 2.5 film.

The story takes place in one coherent, quiet residential neighborhood in Tehran, Iran. All ten 30-second videos must feel like different connected sections of the same physical neighborhood.

The environment is a continuity asset. It must not be redesigned between videos.

## Core Environment Identity

Create a realistic, lived-in but quiet Tehran residential district with:

- Low- to mid-rise residential buildings.
- Practical Iranian urban architecture.
- Narrow and medium-width residential streets.
- Concrete and masonry walls.
- Sidewalks with realistic Iranian curb and paving details.
- Parked ordinary passenger cars.
- Building entrances and gates.
- Utility pipes, cables and rooftop infrastructure.
- Flat concrete rooftops.
- Rooftop water tanks and ventilation equipment.
- Small amounts of ordinary residential vegetation where appropriate.
- No tourist landmarks.
- No iconic skyline.
- No busy commercial district.
- No crowds.
- No dramatic city-center architecture.

The neighborhood should feel believable, ordinary and quiet rather than staged or futuristic.

## Time, Weather and Lighting

The entire film takes place during the same daytime period.

Preserve:

- Sunny clear daytime.
- Consistent sun direction.
- Consistent approximate sun height.
- Consistent shadow direction.
- Consistent weather.
- Natural daylight color.
- Stable atmospheric conditions.
- No transition to sunset, evening or night.
- No sudden cloud cover.
- No rain, snow, fog or storm.
- No seasonal change.

Lighting continuity is mandatory. A later video must not look as though it was filmed on a different day.

## Architecture

Use a consistent architectural language across the neighborhood:

- Light concrete, plaster, brick and masonry surfaces.
- Flat-roof residential buildings.
- Simple balconies and windows.
- Metal gates and practical doors.
- Low boundary walls.
- Functional rooftop structures.
- Realistic utility installations.
- Slightly varied but compatible building facades.
- Natural signs of everyday use without making the neighborhood look abandoned.

Avoid:

- Luxury mansions.
- Glass skyscrapers.
- European-style streets.
- American suburban houses.
- Futuristic buildings.
- Historical monuments.
- Tourist landmarks.
- Oversized apartment towers.
- Visually inconsistent architecture between shots.

## Street and Sidewalk Design

The primary street should contain:

- A realistic residential asphalt road.
- Standard Iranian-style curbs.
- Concrete, stone or tiled sidewalk sections.
- Occasional small irregularities consistent with a real residential street.
- Parked passenger vehicles positioned naturally along the street.
- Enough open space for the child and panda to move safely.
- Side streets that logically branch from the primary street.

The road layout must remain spatially consistent.

A parked car used in one scene should remain recognizable if it appears again later.

## Vehicles

Vehicles are environmental continuity elements.

Use ordinary parked passenger cars appropriate to Tehran.

Preserve:

- General vehicle positions when reused.
- Vehicle size and orientation.
- Roadside parking logic.
- Consistent lighting and reflections.

Do not introduce random traffic or large numbers of moving vehicles.

No vehicle should suddenly appear in a location where the previous scene established that the road was empty.

Vehicles must remain background/environmental elements unless explicitly used in the story.

## Connected Location Map

All story locations belong to the same neighborhood and must connect logically.

### Location 01: Main Residential Street

The opening area.

Characteristics:

- Quiet residential street.
- Sidewalk.
- Parked cars.
- Residential building fronts.
- Enough space for the initial ice-cream scene and the beginning of the chase.

Video 01 establishes the visual identity of the neighborhood.

### Location 02: Side Street

A connected street branching from the main residential street.

Characteristics:

- Similar buildings and sidewalks.
- Same daylight.
- Parked vehicles.
- Slightly narrower movement space.
- Leads naturally toward the vehicle route and rooftop access.

This is used primarily during Video 02.

### Location 03: Vehicle Route and Low Wall

A section containing:

- Parked cars.
- A low concrete or masonry wall.
- A practical opening or route around the wall.
- Access toward a building and rooftop staircase.

The wall must be physically believable and remain the same structure when revisited.

This area is used primarily during Video 03.

### Location 04: Rooftop Staircase

A realistic residential staircase leading from the building level to a flat rooftop.

Characteristics:

- Concrete steps.
- Simple walls or parapets.
- Natural daylight.
- Practical residential construction.
- Enough width for the panda and child to move through separately.

The staircase must remain identical during its appearance in the story.

### Location 05: Flat Rooftop

A connected residential rooftop.

Include:

- Low concrete parapets.
- Ventilation units.
- Pipes.
- Rooftop water tanks.
- Practical utility structures.
- Realistic concrete surfaces.
- Safe connected roof sections where required by the chase.

The rooftop must never become a fantasy action-movie environment.

### Location 06: Street-Level Descent Area

The route from the rooftop/building area back to the residential street.

It must connect naturally to the previously established neighborhood.

No teleportation or unexplained geographical jump is allowed.

### Location 07: Narrow Residential Alley

A progressively narrower residential alley.

Characteristics:

- Concrete or masonry walls.
- Residential doors or gates where appropriate.
- Limited width.
- Realistic pavement.
- Same daylight and architectural language.
- Enough space for the child and panda to move through safely.

The alley gradually leads toward the dead end.

### Location 08: Dead-End Alley

The final section of the narrow alley.

Characteristics:

- Concrete wall blocking the end.
- Two side walls creating a confined space.
- Realistic ground surface.
- No hidden exit.
- Same architectural materials as the surrounding neighborhood.

This location is used for the comedic confrontation in Video 06.

The lightweight wall section that collapses must be visually distinguishable as a temporary or weak non-load-bearing section so the collapse remains believable and safe.

### Location 09: Open Intersection

After the alley, the route returns to a small open residential intersection.

Characteristics:

- Same neighborhood.
- Same street materials.
- Same sunlight.
- Parked vehicles.
- Enough space for the second chase and final reconciliation.

This area is used primarily in Video 08 and Video 09.

### Location 10: Home/Building Entrance

The final destination.

The entrance should feel like it belongs to the same residential neighborhood and should logically connect to the previous street.

Keep:

- Same architectural style.
- Same daylight.
- Same materials.
- A believable residential entrance.
- No sudden change in building quality or neighborhood style.

The child reaches this area at the end of Video 10.

## Spatial Continuity

The ten videos must represent a continuous journey through the same physical neighborhood.

Required route:

Main residential street → side street → parked-car route → low wall → rooftop staircase → rooftop → descent route → residential street → narrow alley → dead-end alley → street → open intersection → home entrance.

Do not skip between locations without a visible or logically implied route.

If a location is revisited, preserve its geometry.

## Rooftop Continuity

The rooftop is part of the same building system established before the chase reaches it.

Preserve:

- Parapet height.
- Staircase position.
- Water tank placement.
- Pipes.
- Ventilation units.
- Roof surface.
- Connected roof sections.
- Building height relative to the surrounding street.

Do not change the rooftop layout between shots.

## Alley Continuity

The narrow alley must progressively become more confined as the chase moves toward the dead end.

Preserve:

- Wall height.
- Wall materials.
- Ground surface.
- Door and gate placement.
- Width of the passage.
- Dead-end wall position.

The dead-end wall must remain in the same physical location for the confrontation.

## Environmental Scale

The environment must maintain believable scale relative to the child and panda.

The one-year-old child is physically small.

The plush panda is approximately the child's height but substantially wider.

Buildings, cars, walls, doors, stairs and sidewalks must retain realistic human-scale proportions.

Never enlarge or shrink the environment to accommodate an action shot.

## Environmental Motion

Keep background motion restrained.

Allowed:

- Subtle tree or plant movement.
- Natural lighting changes caused by camera movement.
- Very limited distant urban activity.

Avoid:

- Crowds.
- Random pedestrians.
- Random animals.
- Heavy traffic.
- Sudden vehicles.
- Large groups.
- Unexplained environmental events.

The quiet neighborhood is an intentional part of the story.

## Camera and Environment Relationship

Camera movement may be dynamic, but the environment itself must remain stable.

Use:

- Low child-height tracking shots.
- Street-level pursuit shots.
- Controlled wide shots.
- Rooftop tracking.
- Controlled overhead or elevated views when necessary.
- Smooth cinematic camera movement.

Do not use camera movement that makes the geography impossible to understand.

## Materials and Realism

Use photorealistic materials:

- Concrete.
- Plaster.
- Brick.
- Asphalt.
- Stone or tile sidewalks.
- Painted metal.
- Glass.
- Realistic vehicle surfaces.
- Natural rooftop materials.

Surfaces should have believable texture, wear and reflections.

Avoid overly clean artificial environments.

## Color and Visual Continuity

Maintain a consistent cinematic visual treatment:

- Natural daylight.
- Realistic skin and material rendering.
- Natural shadows.
- Consistent contrast.
- Consistent exposure.
- Realistic reflections.
- Photorealistic depth of field.
- No sudden color-grade changes.

The environment must visually belong to the same film from Video 01 through Video 10.

4. Same sunlight direction.
5. Same architectural language.
6. Same street materials.
7. Same rooftop design.
8. Same alley geometry.
9. Same dead-end wall.
10. Same home/building entrance.
11. Same general vehicle environment.
12. No unexplained location jumps.
13. No random landmarks.
14. No crowds.
15. No environment redesign.
16. No teleportation.
17. No sudden season or weather change.
18. No nighttime transition.
19. No fantasy architecture.
20. No visible generated text or logos.

## Hard Negative Environment Constraints

Do not generate:

- Different city or country.
- Tourist landmarks.
- Famous Tehran landmarks.
- Crowded streets.
- Commercial shopping districts.
- Highways.
- Futuristic architecture.
- Rural landscapes.
- Desert landscapes.
- European suburban architecture.
- American suburban architecture.
- Heavy traffic.
- Random pedestrians.
- Random animals.
- Sudden rain or snow.
- Night scenes.
- Sunset scenes.
- Changing seasons.
- Duplicate buildings that contradict established geography.
- Impossible roads or staircases.
- Floating structures.
- Teleportation between locations.
- Inconsistent shadows.
- Inconsistent building scale.
- Cartoon or stylized environments.

## Final Environment Standard

Every frame should look as though the camera is physically moving through one real, continuous, quiet Tehran residential neighborhood during the same sunny day.

The environment must support the story without competing with the child and panda.

Continuity is more important than visual novelty.
